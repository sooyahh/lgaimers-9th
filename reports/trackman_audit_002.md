# TM-AUDIT-002 Contextual Trackman Signal

- generated by: `scripts/run_trackman_audit_002.py`
- baseline experiment context: EXP-004 (CatBoost); TM-002A REJECT is the direct predecessor
- data: `data/raw/trackman_history.csv` only (history table, no player-ID join, no hand mapping, no test.csv, no 2025)
- this is an **audit**, not a model experiment: no training, no submission, no new model feature built

## 1. Goal

Answer one question:

> Does Trackman physics vary by **main-shared context** (count / outs / inning) **beyond** the global pitch-group mean, and is that variation **stable across temporal cutoffs** 2022 / 2023 / 2024?

If yes, a controlled contextual Trackman feature is worth one experiment. If no, contextual summary features stay low priority.

## 2. Why this audit

TM-002A added `tm_expected_rel_speed` = (single **global** prior-season pitch-group mean rel_speed) × (current-row as-of mix). It was REJECT: it correlated **+0.98** with `asof_pitcher_fastball_rate`, i.e. a linear remix of an existing feature, and CV mean raw BSS worsened.

The unexamined possibility: TM-002A used a **global constant per group**. It never asked whether the historical physics profile *differs across context that the main row also carries*. If the `breaking`-group IVB profile or the `fastball`-group `rel_speed` profile differs by count in a way that is stable over time, then a **context-conditioned** profile carries variation a global constant cannot, and that a pure mix remix cannot. That is exactly what this audit measures (as an **observational association**, not a causal claim — Section 12.5) — and it deliberately separates *"does the profile differ across context and is that stable"* (this audit) from *"does an engineered feature beat the tree's own count×mix splits"* (a later experiment).

## 5. Physics variables

Trackman schema is the source of truth. Priority: **IVB** (`induced_vert_break`) and **HB** (`horz_break`); diagnostics: `rel_speed`, `spin_rate`, `extension`. Pitch groups split into `fastball / breaking / offspeed`; **`other` excluded** so context differences are not confounded with pitch composition. Core analysis unit = `context × pitch_type_group × physical_variable`, per cutoff.

**Units.** No in-repo source (README, `docs/GPT_HANDOFF.md`, EDA, config, competition schema copy) documents the physical unit of `rel_speed`, `induced_vert_break`, or `horz_break`. The raw fastball `rel_speed` scale (~140–143) is inconsistent with mph, but this audit does **not** assert km/h (or any unit) from the numeric range alone. All physical values below are reported in **raw, unit-less rel_speed / IVB / HB units** exactly as stored. If the official data description later fixes a unit, only the labels change, not the analysis.

Highlights (all cutoffs; ρ = Spearman of count-rank of the mean across cutoff pairs):

| pitch group | variable | effect_ratio (mean) | max\|dev vs global\| | ρ(22,23) | ρ(23,24) | ρ(22,24) | min cell n |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| breaking | IVB | **0.689** | 9.34 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 375 |
| fastball | rel_speed | 0.342 | 1.08 | 0.99 | 0.99 | 0.98 | 10,137 |
| fastball | spin_rate | 0.246 | 27.8 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 10,137 |
| breaking | HB | 0.214 | 3.01 | 0.99 | 0.99 | 0.97 | 375 |
| fastball | IVB | 0.171 | 1.84 | 1.00 | 0.99 | 0.99 | 10,137 |
| fastball | HB | 0.056 | 0.81 | 0.96 | 0.96 | 0.94 | 10,137 |

The headline is **count × breaking × IVB** (2024 cutoff means by count):

| count | 0-0 | 0-1 | 0-2 | 1-0 | 1-1 | 1-2 | 2-0 | 2-1 | 2-2 | 3-0 | 3-1 | 3-2 |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| breaking IVB | 1.45 | -3.67 | -3.38 | 6.50 | 0.95 | -3.28 | 9.57 | 7.15 | -0.57 | 8.93 | 8.95 | 5.12 |

A strong **observational association** is present between count and the `breaking`-group historical IVB profile: for the broad `breaking` group, the count cells differ by ~13 IVB units — high-IVB in hitter's counts (2-0/3-0/3-1 ≈ +9), negative-IVB with two strikes (0-2/1-2 ≈ −3). The `fastball`-group historical `rel_speed` profile is ~1.3–1.8 units higher in two-strike counts (0-2/1-2/2-2/3-2 highest, 1-0/3-0 lowest). These count-conditioned profiles differ in ways a single global-mean profile discards.

**This is association, not a causal physical change** (see Section 12.5). The broad `breaking` group mixes real pitch subtypes (slider/curve/cutter), and the pitch-subtype composition itself differs by count; the pitcher pool also differs by count. So "count-conditioned breaking IVB differs" does **not** mean the same physical pitch has a different IVB because of the count.

## 10. Global vs contextual deviations

Each cell stores `deviation = context_mean − global_pitch_group_mean` (`global_mean` = context-agnostic mean for that cutoff/group/variable). This is the direct TM-002A comparison: TM-002A is the `deviation ≡ 0` special case.

Largest **priority-variable** deviations from the global group mean:

- **count × breaking × IVB**: up to **9.3 IVB units** from the breaking global mean (global ≈ 0.77; hitter's-count cells ≈ +9 to +10). Enormous relative to the global constant.
- count × fastball × IVB: up to 1.84 units (global ≈ 41.8).
- count × breaking × HB: up to 3.0 units.
- inning × breaking × IVB: up to 1.92 units.

Rel_speed contextual deviations are ~1–1.8 units (count, fastball) — small in absolute terms but the same order as the entire 2019→2024 `rel_speed` trend, and unlike TM-002A they are *not* collinear with mix because they are keyed on count. The breaking-IVB deviation is the clearest evidence that a count-conditioned profile differs from the global-mean profile a single constant would use.

## 11. Temporal stability — expanding cutoffs (weaker evidence)

Per-cell means at each cutoff and the context-rank Spearman across cutoff pairs are in `trackman_context_stability_002.csv` / `..._summary_002.csv`.

- **Ranks look highly stable.** For count context, Spearman is 0.94–1.00 for every priority combination across all three cutoff pairs. count × breaking × IVB is 1.00/1.00/1.00.
- **Levels drift, ranks do not.** Absolute physics shifts with the window (e.g. fastball `rel_speed` rises ~0.5–0.6 units from the <2022 to the <2024 window; breaking IVB drifts down ~0.5–1.0 unit). But the *ordering across context* is preserved.
- **Most unstable priority patterns** are low-effect fastball/breaking **HB** by inning and a couple of `other`-adjacent sparse count cells; these already score low effect_ratio and are not candidates.

**Caveat — this is weak evidence on its own.** The cutoffs are expanding windows (<2022 = 2019–2021, <2023 = 2019–2022, <2024 = 2019–2023), so consecutive cutoffs share the great majority of their rows. A Spearman ≈ 1.00 between <2022 and <2023 is therefore *partly mechanical* (they overlap on 2019–2021). Expanding-cutoff stability must not be read as strong temporal robustness by itself; Section 11b re-checks it on non-overlapping seasons.

Rank stability is the pairwise Spearman of the context-value mean vectors over all 10 season pairs, reported **both** over all common cells **and** restricted to cells with n ≥ 1,000 in both seasons (sparse sensitivity). Outputs: `trackman_context_nonoverlap_002.csv`, `trackman_context_nonoverlap_stability_002.csv`.

## 12. 2023 observations

Connecting to the known 2023 regime break (target rate drops 2022→2023; ball/strike asof rates SIGN_FLIP in FEATURE-STABILITY-AUDIT):

- Adding 2022 into the window (the <2023 cutoff) shifts **absolute** physics levels (`rel_speed` up, breaking IVB down) but **does not reorder** the count/outs/inning structure — this holds on both the expanding cutoffs and, more importantly, the independent 2022-only and 2023-only seasons (Section 11b table).
- The variable whose level moves most across seasons is `rel_speed` (secular rise) and `offspeed spin_rate`; the *contextual ordering* on those is nonetheless preserved season by season.
- **Recorded as a regime-shift clue only.** This audit does **not** claim the Trackman drift explains the 2023 model failure. The contextual Trackman ordering is, if anything, *more* stable across the 2023 break than the main asof-rate features that flipped in FEATURE-STABILITY-AUDIT.

## 12.5. Confounding — contextual pattern ≠ causal physical change

The count/inning associations above are **observational** and must not be read causally. At least four confounders are plausible and are **not** controlled here:

- **Pitch-subtype composition inside the broad `breaking` group.** `breaking` pools slider/curve/cutter-type pitches with very different IVB. If pitchers throw proportionally more high-IVB sliders in hitter's counts and more low-IVB curves with two strikes, the *group* IVB moves even if no individual pitch's physics changed. This is the most likely driver of the count × breaking × IVB pattern.
- **Pitcher composition by count / inning.** The set of pitchers reaching a 3-0 count, or pitching the 7th+ inning, is not the same set as elsewhere; their average physics differs.
- **Game / context composition.** Blowouts, opener usage, and matchup selection differ by context.
- **Unobserved selection.** Trackman rows are not sampled from the same games as main (row counts differ by season; TM-AUDIT-001B), so this is a distribution, not a per-row join.

None of this weakens the audit's actual claim — that a **count-conditioned historical Trackman profile differs from the global-mean profile, stably over time**. It only forbids the causal phrasing "count changes the pitch's IVB." A downstream feature would inject *the profile*, whatever its cause; interpreting *why* the profile differs is out of scope.

## 13. Redundancy risk

The rejected TM-002A had HIGH redundancy: `mix × global constant` ⇒ 0.98 correlation with a mix rate. Contextual profiles change the picture per context type:

- **count-conditioned** profile: a feature `Σ_g mix_g · physics_hist(count, g)` varies with **both** the current count **and** the mix. It is **not** a monotone function of any single mix rate (breaking IVB even reverses sign across counts), so it avoids the TM-002A near-clone failure mode. But it is **not "completely new orthogonal information."** The honest framing: it is a **handcrafted contextual feature that injects a Trackman historical physics profile into the existing count × pitch-mix interaction.** CatBoost already holds `count` and the three mix rates, so a tree can already learn *some* of this interaction directly. Residual redundancy risk is therefore **MEDIUM**. The one thing the main data cannot supply is the **external physics magnitude** (main has no IVB at all); whether feeding that pre-formed profile beats the tree's own count×mix splits is **UNKNOWN** and is exactly what a model experiment — not this audit — would decide.
- **outs / inning / top_bottom**: HIGH redundancy or NULL. Outs/inning differences are small and the contexts are already strong main features; top_bottom is null.

So exactly one context type — **count** — clears the bar of "produces a real, stable, non-single-mix-remix profile," while still carrying MEDIUM redundancy against a tree that already sees count and mix.

## 15. Leakage / temporal safety

- Trackman filtered to `season < valid_year` for every cutoff; `assert_trackman_temporal_safety` passed; no `season ≥ valid_year`; 2025 never loaded.
- No pitcher/batter ID join; no hand mapping. Columns used: `season, pitch_type_group, balls_before, strikes_before, outs_before, inning, top_bottom` + physics.
- No current-pitch feature was created; this is an aggregate profile audit, so there is no per-row main feature and no as-of concern here (the *next* experiment must still use the current row's official as-of mix only).
- `test.csv` not loaded. No existing EXP/FE/TM artifact modified. No model trained.
- Non-leakage caveat (unchanged from TM-AUDIT-001B): Trackman rows are not sampled from the same games as main (row counts differ by season). This is distribution context, not a row join.

## 16. Verdict

**`CONTEXTUAL_TM_PROMISING`** (held after the stronger non-overlapping re-check).

The verdict was **not** auto-carried from the expanding-cutoff pass. It was re-examined against the independent-season test (Section 11b), sparse-cell removal, and the causal/redundancy caveats:

- A count-conditioned historical Trackman **profile differs materially from the global-mean profile**, concentrated in **count**. The `breaking`-group IVB profile differs by ~13 units across counts and the `fastball`-group `rel_speed` profile by ~1.3–1.8 units (raw units; unit undocumented in-repo).
- The count structure is **stable across fully independent seasons**, not just overlapping cutoffs: median pairwise Spearman 0.965 for count × breaking × IVB (0.976 after removing the sparse 3-ball cells) and 0.969 for count × fastball × rel_speed, with the structure preserved in 2022-only and 2023-only.
- It is **not** the TM-002A failure mode: the count-conditioned profile is not a monotone function of any single mix rate (breaking IVB reverses sign across counts).
- **Caveats that keep this from being "strong-plus":** the pattern is observational (likely driven by pitch-subtype/pitcher composition within the broad `breaking` group — Section 12.5), and the resulting feature carries MEDIUM redundancy against a tree that already sees count and mix (Section 13). The actual BSS gain is UNKNOWN.

Outs, inning, and top_bottom are weak-to-null and are not pursued. Only count × breaking × IVB (primary) and count × fastball × rel_speed (secondary) are supported.

This authorizes exactly **one** controlled feature experiment — not a Trackman bundle, not multi-variable, not clustering.

## 17. Next single experiment

**`TM-003A` — count-conditioned expected IVB (single feature).**

Framing (important): this is **not "completely new orthogonal information."** It is a **handcrafted contextual feature that injects a Trackman historical physics profile into the existing count × pitch-mix interaction** that CatBoost already partially sees. The actual BSS gain is **UNKNOWN**; MEDIUM redundancy risk applies (Section 13).

- Feature: `tm_expected_ivb_by_count` = `Σ_g asof_mix_g · IVB_hist(count, g)` over `g ∈ {fastball, breaking, offspeed}`, where `IVB_hist(count, g)` is the prior-season (`season < valid_year`) Trackman mean `induced_vert_break` for that count × group. Exactly +1 feature over EXP-004; nothing dropped.
- Why IVB and why count: IVB is a priority variable, main data contains no movement information at all, and count × breaking × IVB is the strongest and most independent-season-stable pattern in this audit.
- **Fallback policy must be fixed before implementation (record only, do not implement):** the `count × group` cells are far thinner than TM-002A's global-per-group means (e.g. 3-0 breaking n ≈ 102–276 per season; the smallest prior-season `count × group` cell must be checked at build time). Decide the fallback **before** coding — e.g. (a) minimum-n per `count × group` cell, below which back off to that group's **count-agnostic** prior-season mean; and (b) cold-start `asof_pitcher_pitchmix_n == 0` → NaN → fold-local median impute, exactly as TM-002A. The fallback rule and its min-n must be frozen up front, not tuned against CV.
- Built-in falsification (within the same run, diagnostic only): also compute the **count-agnostic** `tm_expected_ivb_global` (TM-002A structure with IVB) and report the correlation of the count-conditioned feature with (i) each as-of mix rate and (ii) the count-agnostic version. If the count-conditioned feature is ≈ a mix rate or ≈ its own global version, it is the TM-002A trap again and should be REJECT regardless of CV.
- Guardrails: EXP-004 CatBoost recipe, seed 42, same season weights, expanding 2022/2023/2024 holdouts, `season < valid_year` Trackman only, official as-of mix only, no player ID, no hand, no 2025, no current-pitch TM, no test aggregates, no Public LB. Single feature, single seed. A negative result does not condemn Trackman; a positive result does not authorize a bundle.

**Do not** auto-implement TM-003A or any other feature from this audit. This audit records the candidate and its required fallback policy only, and stops.
